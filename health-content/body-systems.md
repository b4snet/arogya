# Body Systems and Spaceflight

Arogya is a decision-support prototype. It provides general, non-clinical information and demonstration alerts; it does not diagnose or treat medical conditions.

NASA identifies spaceflight risks including altered immune responses, cardiovascular adaptations, inadequate food and nutrition, sleep loss, behavioral changes, bone changes, and reduced musculoskeletal fitness. [web:1][web:2]

## Heart and cardiovascular system

**What it does:** The heart pumps blood through blood vessels to deliver oxygen and nutrients and remove waste products.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Weightlessness changes fluid distribution and blood circulation, and can contribute to cardiovascular deconditioning. NASA identifies cardiovascular adaptation as a human-spaceflight risk. [web:1][web:2]

**Arogya indicators:** Resting heart rate, blood pressure, exercise minutes, fatigue, shortness of breath, dizziness.

**Green:** “Cardiovascular indicators are within the selected demonstration range. Continue your planned activity and monitoring.”

**Yellow:** “One or more cardiovascular indicators are near the demonstration threshold. Rest if needed, hydrate if appropriate, and repeat the measurement later.”

**Orange:** “Multiple cardiovascular indicators are outside the demonstration range. Reduce strenuous activity, rest, and contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Cardiovascular indicators and symptoms suggest urgent review. Stop strenuous activity, rest, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control as needed.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Follow the prescribed exercise plan, adjust intensity rather than pushing through unusual symptoms, rest, drink water if appropriate, repeat measurements later, and contact the crew medical lead when concerned.

## Bones

**What it does:** Bones provide structural support, protect organs, store minerals, and work with muscles to enable movement.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** In microgravity, weight-bearing bones experience reduced loading and can lose mineral density. NASA reports average losses of about 1% to 1.5% of bone mineral density per month during spaceflight without appropriate countermeasures. [web:10][web:13]

**Arogya indicators:** Exercise minutes, pain, nutrition adherence, fatigue.

**Green:** “Bone-support indicators are consistent with the mission plan. Continue prescribed exercise and nutrition.”

**Yellow:** “Exercise or nutrition adherence is below the selected demonstration target. Resume the planned routine when able and record the next check.”

**Orange:** “Repeated missed exercise or nutrition targets may affect bone-support goals. Discuss adjustments with the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Persistent pain, inability to bear weight, or major deviation from the mission plan requires prompt crew medical review.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Complete prescribed resistance and aerobic exercise when cleared to do so, follow the mission nutrition plan, avoid self-directed high-impact activity after injury, rest as needed, and report persistent pain to the crew medical lead.

## Muscles and mobility

**What it does:** Muscles produce movement, maintain posture, support joints, and help regulate balance and coordination.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Reduced gravitational loading can reduce muscle mass, strength, and endurance, especially in muscles used for standing and moving on Earth. Exercise is used as a countermeasure. [web:1][web:10][web:13]

**Arogya indicators:** Exercise minutes, fatigue, pain, dizziness, mood.

**Green:** “Mobility and activity indicators are within the selected demonstration range. Maintain the prescribed exercise routine.”

**Yellow:** “Activity is below the selected demonstration target or fatigue is elevated. Reduce intensity if needed and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Persistent fatigue, pain, or substantially reduced activity may affect mobility goals. Contact the crew medical lead for guidance.”

**Red:** “New weakness, severe pain, dizziness, or inability to perform required movement needs urgent crew medical review.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Follow prescribed exercise, adjust intensity to ability and symptoms, rest, use safe mobility support when available, and discuss persistent limitations with the crew medical lead.

## Immune system

**What it does:** The immune system helps recognize and respond to pathogens and other threats.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Spaceflight stressors can alter immune responses; NASA notes that latent viruses can reactivate and that immune function may change during and after spaceflight. [web:1][web:2][web:10]

**Arogya indicators:** Body temperature, symptoms, sleep duration, fatigue, nutrition adherence, mood.

**Green:** “No selected immune-related warning indicators are active. Continue routine hygiene, nutrition, sleep, and monitoring.”

**Yellow:** “A symptom or recovery indicator is outside the selected demonstration range. Rest, hydrate if appropriate, and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Multiple symptoms or persistent changes are present. Reduce nonessential exertion and contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Fever, breathing difficulty, severe symptoms, or rapidly worsening condition requires urgent crew medical review and possible mission-control notification.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Rest, drink water if appropriate, follow the mission nutrition plan, use relaxation or mindfulness activities, talk with a crew member, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control if needed.

## Sleep and mental wellbeing

**What it does:** Sleep supports recovery, attention, mood, decision-making, and physical health. Mental wellbeing includes mood, stress, connection, and ability to cope with demands.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Isolation, confinement, workload, noise, altered light cycles, and circadian disruption can contribute to sleep loss, fatigue, and behavioral or performance changes. [web:1][web:10][web:13]

**Arogya indicators:** Sleep duration, mood, fatigue, anxiety, low mood, exercise minutes.

**Green:** “Sleep and wellbeing indicators are within the selected demonstration range. Maintain regular rest and crew connection.”

**Yellow:** “Sleep, mood, or fatigue is outside the selected demonstration range. Prioritize rest, reduce avoidable workload where possible, and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Persistent poor sleep, low mood, anxiety, or fatigue may affect performance. Use a relaxation activity and contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Safety concerns, severe distress, or inability to perform essential tasks require immediate contact with the crew medical lead or mission control.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Follow the sleep schedule, rest, use relaxation or mindfulness activity, adjust exercise intensity, talk with a crew member, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control if needed.

## Nutrition and metabolism

**What it does:** Nutrition provides energy and nutrients for physical activity, tissue maintenance, immune function, bone health, and cognition.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Inadequate food and nutrition can affect crew health and performance. NASA identifies inadequate food and nutrition as a spaceflight risk and emphasizes balanced, nutritious mission food systems. [web:1][web:2][web:13]

**Arogya indicators:** Blood sugar, body temperature, hydration, fatigue, exercise minutes, nutrition adherence.

**Green:** “Nutrition and energy indicators are within the selected demonstration range. Continue the mission nutrition plan.”

**Yellow:** “Food, fluid, or energy indicators are outside the selected demonstration range. Follow the mission nutrition plan and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Repeated low intake, dehydration concerns, or persistent fatigue may affect performance. Contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Inability to eat or drink, repeated low blood sugar readings, or severe symptoms require urgent crew medical review.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Follow the mission nutrition plan, drink water if appropriate, rest, adjust exercise intensity, repeat measurements later, and contact the crew medical lead if needed.

## Eyes and vision

**What it does:** The eyes detect light and support vision, depth perception, reading, navigation, and task performance.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Some astronauts report vision changes during long-duration missions, including changes in near-distance visual acuity. NASA continues to study the causes and countermeasures. [web:7][web:10]

**Arogya indicators:** Vision changes, headache, dizziness, blood pressure, fatigue.

**Green:** “No vision-related warning indicators are active. Continue routine monitoring.”

**Yellow:** “Mild or temporary visual discomfort is reported. Rest, avoid unnecessary visual strain, and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Persistent or worsening vision changes, headache, or dizziness should be reviewed by the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain, major new visual disturbance, or associated neurological symptoms require urgent crew medical review.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Rest, reduce unnecessary visual strain, repeat a measurement or self-check later, avoid operating safety-critical equipment if vision is impaired, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control if needed.

## Radiation exposure

**What it does:** Radiation exposure refers to energy from space radiation that can interact with body tissues. NASA monitors exposure and studies ways to reduce risk.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Space radiation exposure differs from Earth exposure and can have short- and long-term health consequences depending on total dose and timing. NASA identifies radiation-induced cancer risk as a human-spaceflight risk. [web:1][web:10][web:15]

**Arogya indicators:** Mission radiation-exposure record, fatigue, nausea, headache, symptoms.

**Green:** “Radiation exposure is within the selected demonstration monitoring range. Continue standard mission procedures.”

**Yellow:** “Reported exposure or symptoms are near the selected demonstration threshold. Follow mission procedures and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Exposure or symptoms exceed the selected demonstration threshold. Reduce nonessential exposure where possible and contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “A major exposure event or severe symptoms require immediate crew medical review and mission-control notification.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Follow mission radiation-protection procedures, rest, drink water if appropriate, record symptoms and exposure information, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control if needed.

## Overall wellbeing

**What it does:** Overall wellbeing reflects the combined status of physical health, sleep, nutrition, activity, mood, symptoms, and ability to perform mission tasks.

**How spaceflight can affect it:** Spaceflight combines altered gravity, radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, and hostile or closed environments. These hazards can affect physiology, behavior, and performance. [web:1][web:7][web:15]

**Arogya indicators:** All Arogya indicators, especially sleep duration, mood, fatigue, hydration, exercise minutes, symptoms, and vital-sign measurements.

**Green:** “Overall wellbeing indicators are within the selected demonstration range. Continue the planned routine.”

**Yellow:** “One or more wellbeing indicators need attention. Rest, hydrate if appropriate, follow the mission plan, and repeat the check later.”

**Orange:** “Several indicators are outside the selected demonstration range. Reduce avoidable workload, use a relaxation activity, and contact the crew medical lead.”

**Red:** “Multiple severe indicators or safety concerns require immediate contact with the crew medical lead or mission control.”

**Safe recommended actions:** Rest, drink water if appropriate, follow the mission nutrition and exercise plan, use relaxation or mindfulness activity, talk with a crew member, and contact the crew medical lead or mission control if needed.
