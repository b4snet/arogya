# Spaceflight Hazards

NASA groups human-spaceflight hazards into five categories: radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, changes in gravity, and hostile or closed environments. [web:1][web:7][web:15]

| Hazard | Potential effect | Arogya monitoring focus | Safe general response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Altered gravity | Fluid shifts, cardiovascular adaptation, bone and muscle loss, balance and vision changes | Heart rate, blood pressure, exercise, pain, dizziness, vision changes | Follow prescribed exercise and nutrition plans; rest and adjust intensity as needed. [web:1][web:10][web:13] |
| Radiation | Higher space-radiation exposure than on Earth; potential short- and long-term health effects | Exposure record, fatigue, nausea, headache, symptoms | Follow mission radiation-protection procedures and report concerns to the crew medical lead. [web:1][web:10] |
| Isolation and confinement | Stress, reduced privacy, interpersonal strain, sleep disruption, mood changes | Sleep duration, mood, fatigue, anxiety, low mood | Maintain rest routines, use relaxation activities, and talk with crew members. [web:1][web:10][web:13] |
| Distance from Earth | Delayed communication and limited immediate external support | Symptoms, urgent alerts, crew medical lead contact | Use established communication and escalation procedures. [web:1][web:15] |
| Hostile or closed environment | Environmental stressors such as noise, temperature, carbon dioxide, and limited living space | Sleep, fatigue, symptoms, hydration, temperature | Follow habitat procedures, rest when possible, and report persistent issues. [web:10][web:13] |

## Health risks identified by NASA

- Altered immune responses. [web:1][web:2]
- Cardiovascular adaptations. [web:1][web:2]
- Inadequate food and nutrition. [web:1][web:2]
- Sleep loss, circadian disruption, and work overload. [web:1][web:3]
- Behavioral and performance changes. [web:1][web:7]
- Spaceflight-induced bone changes and fracture risk. [web:1][web:2][web:3]
- Reduced cardiorespiratory and musculoskeletal fitness. [web:1][web:2][web:3]
- Radiation-induced cancer risk. [web:1][web:3][web:15]

## Arogya approach

Arogya uses locally stored, self-reported and manually entered information to organize general health observations. Its color levels are demonstration thresholds, not clinical diagnostic criteria.

Arogya supports safe, general actions only: rest, appropriate hydration, repeating a measurement, adjusting exercise intensity, following mission nutrition plans, relaxation or mindfulness, talking with a crew member, and contacting the crew medical lead or mission control when needed.
