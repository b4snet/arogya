# Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Arogya | A decision-support prototype created for the NASA Space Apps Challenge. |
| Blood pressure | The pressure of blood against artery walls, expressed as systolic over diastolic pressure in mmHg. |
| bpm | Beats per minute; the unit used for heart rate. |
| Cardiovascular system | The heart and blood vessels, which circulate blood throughout the body. |
| Circadian rhythm | The body’s roughly 24-hour internal cycle that influences sleep, alertness, and other functions. |
| Crew medical lead | The designated crew member responsible for coordinating medical support and escalation procedures. |
| Demonstration threshold | A value used in the prototype to trigger a color-coded alert; it is not a validated clinical threshold. |
| Fatigue | A self-reported feeling of tiredness, reduced energy, or reduced capacity for activity. |
| Hydration | The body’s fluid status, represented in Arogya by a 1–5 self-report scale. |
| Microgravity | A condition of very reduced apparent gravity, such as that experienced in orbit. |
| Mission control | The ground team that supports mission operations, communications, and crew safety. |
| mmHg | Millimeters of mercury; the unit used for blood pressure. |
| mg/dL | Milligrams per deciliter; the unit used for blood glucose in this prototype. |
| Resting heart rate | Heart rate measured while awake and at rest. |
| Spaceflight hazard | A condition of spaceflight that can affect health or performance, including radiation, isolation and confinement, distance from Earth, altered gravity, and hostile or closed environments. [web:1][web:7] |
| Symptom | A self-reported physical or mental experience, such as headache, dizziness, nausea, pain, shortness of breath, vision changes, anxiety, or low mood. |
| Wellbeing | A broad view of physical health, sleep, nutrition, activity, mood, symptoms, and ability to perform mission tasks. |
